# 1. Historical overview and context
## 1.1 historic usage of hydrogen to combine theory and reality
Historically the hydrogen atom gave a deep insight into the interactions on the smallest level. With its relatively simple structure it was relatevely easy to describe it in mathematical equations. It being an almost perfect two level system could be proven experimentally and then described mathematecally. From the earliest description of the energy levels by Bohr, up to the hyperfine structure, which implemented ground breaking ideas of quantum physics, the hydrogen atom was used to give experimental proof. 
The transition frequency is a measure of the "distance" between two levels of the hydrogen two level system. An electron gets exctied into a higher, in this case the second, level and then get's emmited via the stimulated emission. It is called "stimulated", because a laser with the transition frequency is used to bring the electron to the base system. During this, the electron looses a photon, which we can see. It means that if we get the laser as precise as possible, the exact transition frequency will be known.

## 1.2 proton puzzle
Using the transition frequency scientist can determine the size of the nucleus of a hydrogen. The nucleus in this case being the proton. Over the last decades the proton radius was measured by a multitude of scientific groups and up to date the most presice one is the value that was gotten from the muonic hydrogen. Because of its heavier, the electrons orbit the proton at a closer distance. This negaties some of the quantum effects and also is not dependand on the Rydberg constant. Other measurements done on normal hyrogen showed not only much larger error bars, but the value also did not much, even inside the error bar interval. The groups measuring the proton on normal hydrogen after, were able to come cloer to the muonic level with each step, until the proton puzzle was solved this year, but a group in the MPQ. Their value coinsides with the muonic one to a never seen before presision, proving that the muonic measurement also gave the correct proton radius value. Otherwise there would have been the question of whether there might be some physics that was overseen before.

# 2. 1S-3S experiment itself  
The 1S-3S group is working with the transition 1s to 3S . Using a TiSa laser they excite the hydrogen atoms inside the hydrogen beam. The atomic hydrogen is created by a plasma, that dissacociates the molecular hydrogen. 
In order to have reliable data, it is important to have at least some and at best consistent amout of atomic hydrogen in the flux. For that purpuse there are multiple ways to do that. One is to use a pressure gauge(?), which shows that there is a difference depending on the ratio of atomic to molecular particles, but due to the working process of a gauge, this isn't a very reliable method, especially considering that plasma (ionized stuff) is used in the setup. ANother way is to take the spectrum of the plasma itself and dedcue form there how high the disaccosiation ratio is, by analysing the Fulcher alpha line. This is a very presice method, but the actual spectroscopy is being done further away form the plasma, meaning that the atmoic hydrogen will recombine on the way there. A very promising approach was to scan the beam as it is leaving the nozzle, right before it is being analyzed. This can be done with the wire detectro, which is the main topic of this thesis.
