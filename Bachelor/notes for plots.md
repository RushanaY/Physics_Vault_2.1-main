# 06.08 pumping hydrogen down
we filled the chamber with hydrogen up to 2 or even 3 mbar and let it pump down with the turbo pump and the normal one over night. 
we wanted to observe the how the resistance changes with changing background pressure (background cooling gas) 
we left the bypass closed and filled that pat full with hydrogen, so that it was leaking onto the front wire over time 
from zooming closer into the area where background pressure of the main chamber changes we can se the following:
- highest background pressure resulted in the smallest resistance (checks out as the background cooling is the strongest)
- as pressure goes down the resistance goes up 
- pressure looks to be going down exponentially, while resistance is more or less linear 
- the difference between fron and backwire goes up in the moment where the pressure goes down, implicating, that the hydrogen beam adds on to the cooling

# 10.08 changing temperature 
the nozzle is very closed to the wire and if their temperatures are different, this has a big impact onto the resistance of the wire. so we turned the nozzle to 380K and then down to 270 roughly. 
Hot nozzle leads to large resistance, cold one leads to small resistance. 
there was still some flow onto the front wire 
Observation of resistance difference 
- when nozzle heats up, the difference goes up => front/back heats more/less (this can be due t the distance difference; or the front wire gets warmed up by the warm hydrogen, that get's thermalised by the nozzle?) 
- when nozzle is stable, both wires become closer in value , thermalising?
- when nozzle cools down -> front wire maybe get's cooled more, or back wire is closer
