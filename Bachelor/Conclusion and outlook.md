# Conclusion 
As in my conclusion I would say, that we were able to to stabilize the signal and minimize the RF source jump form before. We also put into action the DAQ code, which allows to take many more data points to follow the resistance change in real time. The new platform allowes now to see that there is maybe another source of a resistance change, that happens at the same time as the plasma is ignited, but doesn't necceseraly comes form the recombination. The cooled nozzle was implemented and didn't improve our measurements tremendiously, so it wasn't used much. 

# Outlook 
maybe some improvement ideas or stuff we didn't have time to try out. It is based either on our experience or a personal conversation with Dylan Yost. 
Main problem is the quality of the plasma. In this regard there are many things that can be done, which are even sometimes contratigting themselves. It is about the position of the MW cavity. Some groups, like the one in Wien, have experienced better results with the cavity being further away form the orfice, Dylan suggested that as small disance as possible, talking form his own experience. Mainly what is needed, is a very good atomic hydrogen source, which doesn't have to be plasma necceraly. ALso we noticed, that the plasma characteristics change over time, where plasma has been on, how often it was on and off, how much time was between ingitions. We were not able to find a correlation, so the plasma was always the big uncertainty when it came to the quality of the measurements. 
Another way to get more telling results, better contrast between atomic and molecular hydrogen, is by moving the wire closer to the nozzle. In Dylans case it was 1mm, which is 5 times shorter than our case. He was able to take a "cut" of the beam and see its geometry. 
Also what can be tried is a shorter wire, because this might impact the way heat gets conducted? 
At last, an intresting variable to measure, would be the probing current. Both smaller or higher current have arguments to why these might be benefitial. The smaller current will heat the wire less, making it easier to see a small change of a coulple Kelvin on this background temperature. A higher current might increase the number of recombinations and maybe? make the resistance more precise. This will however require a more major rebuilding of the set up, as the used HP multimeter does not allow for a manual setting of the probing current. There it is done automatically, meaning that the "Four wire method" will have to be build out of other power supplies.
