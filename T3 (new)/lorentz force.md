$$\vec f = \rho \vec E + \vec J \times \vec B$$ describes the impact of both E and B fields on matter 

# Derivation 
from equations for [[Energy density]] and [[Momentum density]] we get 4 more c[[continuity equation]]s 
In case of absence of charges and fluxes ($\rho =0, \vec J =0$) we have $$\partial_t \mathcal{E} + \vec \triangledown \cdot \mathcal{S} =0$$
$$\partial_t \mathcal{P}_i - \sum_{j=1}^3 \partial_j \theta_{ij} =0, \qquad i =1,2,3$$

from that derived: the **Conservation of Momentum of the EM field** $$\frac{d}{dt} \int d^3 x \mathcal{P} =0$$
and the Conservation of Energy of the EM field 
$$\frac{d}{dt} \int d^3 x \mathcal{E} =0$$

Any other case ($\rho \neq 0, \vec J \neq 0$) there is exchange between the field and the matter 
It is still important that conservation is remained at least locally, there is only a certain amount of energy that matter can intake from the field per time $$\vec J \cdot \vec E$$
same is for momentum density per time: $$f = \rho \vec E + \vec J \times \vec B$$
